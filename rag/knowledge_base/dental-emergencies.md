## Scope and source

Dental care is one of the levels of in-flight care that NASA programs must plan for [2]. The steps below summarize the ISS medical checklist dental procedures (2015 versions, released through FOIA) [1]. They use the ISS kit; other vehicles may carry different items. Every procedure ends with photo documentation of the tooth and downlink of the photos to the flight surgeon [1].

## Recognition

- **Tooth pain**: toothache, sensitivity to hot, cold or biting.
- **Lost filling**: a hole or rough edge in a tooth, food trapping, sensitivity.
- **Loose or lost crown**: crown comes off intact.
- **Fractured tooth with exposed pulp**: broken tooth with a pink or red spot in the center, often very painful.
- **Lost tooth**: tooth knocked out, bleeding socket.

The checklist includes a mouth exam and mouth anatomy reference images to help describe the location [1].

## Immediate actions (ISS checklist)

**Tooth pain** [1]:
1. Take two ibuprofen 400 mg tablets and one hydrocodone/acetaminophen 10 mg/300 mg tablet for pain control.
2. If pain is severe or immediate relief is needed, perform the dental nerve block procedure.

**Lost filling** [1]:
1. Photograph tooth; put on gloves; remove debris (brushing helps).
2. Roll temporary filling material into a ball, press it into the cavity and shape it; wipe excess.
3. Subject bites firmly for 2 minutes; smooth excess with the dental carver. Initial set in 5 minutes; resume drinking after about 1 hour; avoid hard food on that side for 12-24 hours.

**Loose crown** [1]:
- No pain or sensitivity: store the crown in a bag in a known place (sleep station).
- Pain or sensitivity: clean old cement, check fit, coat the inside with dental adhesive, seat the crown, bite on a cotton swab, then moderate pressure for 2-3 minutes; clean excess cement.

**Exposed pulp** [1]:
1. Photograph and downlink; take the same ibuprofen plus hydrocodone/acetaminophen dose.
2. Consult the surgeon on which nerve block to place and perform it.
3. Moisten gauze with eugenol, squeeze out excess, place over the pulp and hold with the teeth for 1-2 minutes. Avoid eugenol contact with skin or gums (tissue damage).
4. Avoid chewing on that side and keep food out of the cavity.

**Lost tooth** [1]:
1. Apply continuous direct pressure on the socket with gauze until bleeding stops.
2. Same pain medication; nerve block if pain is severe.
3. Bag the tooth, photograph the mouth and downlink.

## In-flight specifics: dental nerve block

The nerve block is used when pain is severe or immediate relief is needed; for exposed pulp the checklist says to consult the surgeon on the type of block [1]:
- benzocaine swabstick on the site for 5-10 minutes first;
- a 10 mL syringe is modified with tape for one-handed aspiration (to check the needle is not in a blood vessel);
- 2-3 mL of lidocaine over the tooth root (upper or lower technique);
- wait 5 minutes and repeat once if pain persists.

Workspace: gray tape to hold tools and ziplock bags for trash, because loose items float [1].

## When to contact the flight surgeon

NASA lists caries, exposed pulp, dental abscess, lost filling, avulsion (knocked-out tooth) and crown loss among the dental conditions programs may need to treat in flight [3].

- Always: send photos after any dental procedure [1].
- Promptly: swelling of the face or jaw, fever, pus, difficulty opening the mouth or swallowing, pain not controlled by the medication above, bleeding that does not stop with pressure, or a tooth injury from facial trauma (check for other injuries).

## Follow-up

- Soft diet on the affected side, oral hygiene, pain log.
- Keep the crewmember's photos in separate folders labeled with name and GMT day [1].
- After a temporary filling, wait about 1 hour before drinking and avoid hard food on that side for 12-24 hours [1].
- Keep a stored crown or tooth bagged in a known place [1].
- Record medication doses and times for the surgeon.
- Temporary repairs need definitive dental care after return; NASA includes preventive dental care in pre-mission health care [3].
