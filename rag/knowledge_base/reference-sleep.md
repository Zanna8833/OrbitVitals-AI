## Measured sleep duration in flight

The largest study (Barger et al., Lancet Neurology 2014) used wrist actigraphy and daily logs in 64 Shuttle astronauts (1,063 flight days) and 21 ISS astronauts (3,248 flight days) [1]:

| Condition | Sleep obtained (mean, actigraphy) |
|---|---|
| Shuttle, in flight | 5.96 h (SD 0.56) |
| ISS, in flight | 6.09 h (SD 0.67) |
| ISS, about 3 months before launch | 6.41 h |
| ISS, first week after landing | 6.95 h |
| Shuttle, first week after landing | 6.74 h |

- Shuttle crew attempted 7.35 h of sleep per night but obtained 5.96 h [1].
- Short sleep was present already during pre-flight training, not only in space [1].
- For comparison, adults generally need 7-9 hours for optimal daytime functioning [2].

Interpretation for monitoring: an ISS crewmember averaging about 6 h per night is typical of published data, but it is still sleep deficiency. Look for a drop below the crewmember's own in-flight average, several consecutive short nights, or worsening subjective sleep quality.

## Sleep medication use

- 78% of Shuttle crewmembers (61 of 78) used a sleep-promoting drug at least once, on 52% of nights; 75% of ISS crewmembers (12 of 16) reported using one [1].
- NASA notes total sleep time was not significantly different with or without hypnotics, although time to fall asleep was about 8.5 minutes shorter [2].
- ISS kit sleep aids (2015 list): melatonin 3 mg, zaleplon 10 mg, zolpidem 10 mg; the kit warns not to combine them with other sleep or sedating medications [4]. Use is per flight surgeon guidance.
- Operational concern: medication taken close to an emergency or a critical task may impair performance; coordinate with the crew so not everyone is sedated at once.

## Why sleep is short in space

NASA lists environmental causes: microgravity, elevated CO2, lighting and light leaks, noise, temperature, humidity, lack of privacy and the location of sleep quarters; ISS crew cite noise, temperature, physical discomfort and mission workload [2]. On ISS, temperature is 18-27 °C and humidity 30-65% [2]. Orbital sunrise and sunset and shifted work schedules disturb circadian rhythms, which NASA manages with light, schedules, melatonin and, when indicated, wake-promoting drugs prescribed by the flight surgeon or fatigue management service [3].

## Performance cost

- After seven days of six hours of sleep, performance is reported to equal that of a person awake for 24-36 hours [2].
- After 36 hours of sleep deprivation, teams shared less information and discussed strategy less [2].
- Circadian desynchrony is associated with inattention, distraction, slowed reaction time and poor decision making, plus metabolic disturbance and sleep disruption [3].

## Red flags for the assistant

- Average sleep falling clearly below the crewmember's own in-flight baseline for several consecutive nights (a monitoring heuristic, not a NASA threshold).
- Rising hypnotic use, or a hypnotic taken before an EVA or critical operation.
- Poor sleep together with headaches or rising cabin ppCO2 (check CO2 near sleep stations).
- Poor sleep together with low mood, irritability or withdrawal (see behavioral health document).
- Recommend reporting persistent insomnia at the weekly private medical conference.
