## How to read this schedule

Timing notation used by NASA: **L-** days or months before launch, **L+** or **FD** (flight day) days after launch, **R-** days before return, **R+** days after return. The values below are the nominal schedules published by NASA for long-duration (more than 30 days) ISS missions; the flight surgeon can add checks at any time "as clinically indicated" [2][3]. Programs tailor schedules to each mission [3].

These are scheduled checks, not alarm thresholds: a symptom never waits for the next scheduled check. Report it and request a private medical conference.

NASA's medical operations brief lists the kinds of in-flight evaluations a program schedules: private medical conferences, private psychological conferences, hearing assessments, periodic health status checks, periodic fitness exams, pre- and post-EVA examinations and pre-landing medical evaluations [7].

## Conferences and EVA-related checks

Private medical conferences (PMC), 15 minutes per crewmember [1]:
- **daily for the first 5 days** after docking;
- **weekly** after that;
- **before each EVA** (within 24 hours of suit donning) and **after each EVA** (within 24 hours of suit removal);
- **daily from 5 days before entry**, and on the morning of entry or landing;
- **at any time** on request of the flight director, commander, any crewmember or crew surgeon.

Private psychological conferences are also scheduled [7].

Cognition: a cognitive test (WinSCAT) is taken **monthly** and compared with the crewmember's pre-flight baseline, with extra tests after injury or illness [6].

## Imaging, eyes and laboratory

- **Eyes (SANS)**: "eye weeks" of 2-3 days of testing including optical coherence tomography on **FD 30, FD 90 and R-30** during 6-month flights [2]. NASA's surveillance schedule lists in-mission eye exams at **L+30, L+90, L+180, L+270 and/or R-30**, covering visual acuity, Amsler grid, contrast sensitivity, fundoscopy, ultrasound, OCT and tonometry [3]. Longer missions add the L+180 and L+270 sessions.
- **Jugular veins (clot screening)**: ultrasound at about **L+30 and L+60 days** and at **R-42 days**, plus pre-flight (L-12 to L-3 months) and post-flight checks [4].
- **Blood and urine in flight**: around **L+180 days** on long missions [4]. Post-flight blood collection at R+0/1, R+3/7 and R+14/30 [3].
- **Body mass** is measured in flight; hearing, nutrition, psychological status, aerobic capacity and microbial and air and water quality are also monitored in flight [3].

## Environment and exposure monitoring

- **Radiation**: personal dosimetry in flight and after flight [3] (see the radiation dose reference).
- **Noise**: crew-worn acoustic dosimeter for 24 hours, done **every 60 days** (about every other month) and compared with ISS flight rules [5] (see the hearing and noise document).
- **Cabin atmosphere**: CO2, O2 and contaminants are monitored by vehicle systems (see the cabin atmosphere reference); air and water quality monitoring is part of in-mission surveillance [3].

## Pre- and post-flight checks (for context)

From NASA's longitudinal health surveillance schedule [3]:
- Eye exams: comprehensive at L-21/18 or L-9/6 months, shorter exam at L-90/30 days; post-flight eye exam immediately after landing and a comprehensive one in the first 1-3 days.
- Laboratory tests: L-90/30 days (blood chemistry, hematology, thyroid, iron, special tests; urinalysis and pregnancy test).
- Functional fitness tests: L-6/9 months, L-90/30 days, R+5/7 and R+30; isokinetic strength at L-9/6 months, L-90/30 days, R+5, R+14 and R+30.
- Balance (dynamic posturography): L-90/30 and R+8 days.

## Notes for the assistant

- Use this schedule to prompt reminders (for example a PMC within 24 hours before EVA, daily PMCs in the 5 days before entry) and to know when the next objective data point (eye exam, ultrasound, labs) will be available [1][2][4].
- Between scheduled checks, rely on the crewmember's own trends (vital signs, sleep, body mass, exercise logs) and on symptoms.
- NASA does not publish fixed in-flight sampling rates for heart rate, blood pressure or SpO2 in these documents; do not invent them.
